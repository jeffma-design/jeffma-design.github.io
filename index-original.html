About

I direct design to close the gap between what experts know and what decisions, products, and people require.

The technical domains vary — cybersecurity, AI safety, privacy, enterprise risk, data architecture — and the industries vary just as widely, from retail and financial services to legal and beyond. The audience is consistently senior. What stays constant is the challenge of making something genuinely difficult land with people who need to understand it, not study it.

Getting ambitious work made inside a large, complex organization is its own discipline — one I’ve built over seven years navigating Google’s scale. It means building alignment across legal, policy, communications, engineering, and product simultaneously, often without direct authority over any of them. I move work forward through clarity of vision, early coalition-building, and knowing which constraints are real and which are negotiable. The work doesn’t just get conceived. It gets shipped.

What I Do

I work at the intersection of three things most organizations keep separate — creative authorship, strategic judgment, and the discipline of communicating to power.

01

Creative direction

Authorship over how an idea is expressed, across any medium. Film, report, presentation, product, system. The medium follows the problem.

02

Design strategy

Shaping what gets made and why, before anyone opens a tool. The decisions that determine whether the work will land before a pixel is placed.

03

Executive communication

Translating what experts know into the clarity that senior audiences need to act. Boards, C-suites, heads of state, global policy forums.

Who I Work With

My clients are organizations where the knowledge gap is wide and the stakes of not closing it are high.

The people who understand the thing are not the people who decide about it. That gap exists everywhere — but it’s most acutely felt in technology, consulting, financial services, defense, policy, and healthcare, where the complexity is real, the audiences are senior, and the cost of being misunderstood is significant.

If your most important ideas aren’t landing with the people who need to act on them — that’s the problem I solve.
