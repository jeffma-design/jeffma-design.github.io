Even brilliant ideas need help landing beyond the room where they were built.

The people closest to an idea are often too deep in it to explain it simply. I help them step back and translate their expertise distinctively for each audience who needs to understand it — internal leaders, governing bodies, boards of directors, or millions of people using a product for the first time.

What I Do

I direct design to close the gap between what experts know and what decisions, products, and people require.

I get there by combining three disciplines — design strategy, creative direction, and high-stakes communication — to bring everyone who needs to understand an idea into alignment before it can move forward. I’m the conduit between them.

01

Design strategy

Shaping what gets made and why, before anyone opens a tool. The decisions that determine whether the work will land before a pixel is placed.

02

Creative direction

Authorship over how an idea is expressed, across any medium. Film, report, presentation, product, system. The medium follows the problem.

03

High-stakes communication

Distilling what experts know into exactly the interpretation each audience needs — boards, C-suites, heads of state, global policy forums.

Work can fall short of its potential not because an idea was wrong, but because the people who needed to understand it never fully did.

Who I Work With

I work with people who recognize a knowledge gap exists and the stakes of not closing it are high.

The people who understand the thing are not the people who decide about it. That gap exists everywhere — but it’s most acute where the complexity is real, the audiences are senior, and the cost of being misunderstood is significant.

That need is usually tied to a specific moment, whatever the size or maturity of the organization: a startup’s first funding round, a scaling company’s first product launch that needs executive buy-in, a regulator that needs a clear explanation, or an internal audience that needs to understand a change before it lands publicly.
