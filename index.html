Why it’s hard

My observation

The closer you are to an idea, the harder it can be to explain.

It’s called the curse of knowledge. Once you know something deeply, what feels obvious to you loses everyone else. An outside eye can help you see what to explain, what to leave out, and how to move audiences.

How audiences get lost

The closer you are to an idea, the harder it can be to explain.

It’s called the curse of knowledge. Once you know something deeply,
what feels obvious to you loses everyone else.

And when you’re closest to the work, it’s easy to

  • stay in the weeds.
  • oversimplify.
  • use jargon.
  • prove how much you know.
  • skip the visuals.
  • leave out steps.
  • bury the ask.
  • assume they already care.

The gap between knowing and explaining can be difficult to bridge. Closing that gap is a different kind of work.

How I help

Conversation to craft

I connect the dots with you, then for your audience.

Design starts with a conversation and guides every step that follows.

  1. Listen

    I listen and line up what you’re saying with what you mean.

  2. Ask

    I ask the questions your audience will, and the ones they’re thinking.

  3. Edit

    I keep what this audience needs and set aside the rest.

  4. Shape

    I tune your story for the people hearing it.

  5. Craft

    Then I design what carries it.

Working together produces three kinds of work.

  • 1

    The Message

    What you know, distilled into a story that holds together

    • Strategy workshops
    • Messaging playbook
    • Core narrative
  • 2

    The Kit

    What you need to tell your story, made for each audience

    • One-pagers and briefs
    • Key visuals and metaphors
    • Presentation templates
  • 3

    The Story

    Finished pieces, made for the people who need them

    • Keynote or pitch
    • Explainer video
    • Full campaign

And here’s what you gain.

  • Faster understanding The point lands in minutes, with the details there when people want them
  • Better conversations Time goes to moving the idea forward, not decoding it
  • A story that travels It sticks, and people pass it on to boards, teams, or the public

Once the thinking is clear, everything that comes after gets easier: every deck, every deck, launch, and conversation.
