Security Analysis and Reporting

Dense threat intelligence, turned into public reports for the Munich Security Conference

About

Google’s senior security analysts had new findings to share, in some cases attributing specific attacks to specific threat actors for the first time. My job was to help them get those findings out into the world at one of the security industry’s biggest moments, the Munich Security Conference.

I worked through their raw data, listened to them talk through what they’d found, and asked questions until I understood what mattered most and what readers would value. They also walked me through how they wanted people to feel reading it. Ideas that are second nature to experts, like data exfiltration, I explained visually, so non-technical readers could see how an attack unfolds and where the weak points are.

I led the design direction and built a design system that internal and external teams could use across every report, and its presentations. Some reports responded to current events and had to land on tight deadlines. Beyond the conference, they were shared in bilateral meetings with national defense departments around the world, earning the trust of important partners and showing Google’s leadership in cybersecurity.
